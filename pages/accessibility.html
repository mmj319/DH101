♿ Accessibility Statement

How accessibility and inclusive design principles are woven into my digital humanities work.

Accessibility Principles in DH101

Digital spaces should be welcoming, accessible, and comfortable for everyone. In designing this portfolio, accessibility is not an afterthought—it is a core creative principle that shapes visual styling, contrast ratios, and structural hierarchy.

🎨 Visual & Contrast Considerations

This website includes both a soft pastel light mode and a deep plum/midnight dark mode. In both modes, color palettes are carefully calibrated for readability and reduced eye strain:

  • Text Contrast: Dark charcoal text (#4a3e47) on creamy backgrounds in light mode, and crisp light text (#f5edf2) on deep plum backgrounds in dark mode.
  • Typography: Friendly, highly legible rounded sans-serif typefaces (Quicksand for headings and Nunito for body copy) chosen for clear letterforms and easy reading.
  • Spacing & Hierarchy: Generous line height (1.7), clear headings, and structured cards to support screen readers and reduce cognitive fatigue.

🏷️ Image Descriptions & Alt Text

Every visual artifact—from Angel Baby's photos to our multi-panel comics and AI selfies—includes comprehensive descriptive alt attributes. This ensures that visitors using screen readers or browsing on text-only clients have full access to visual humor, artistic details, and project context.

⌨️ Keyboard & Motor Navigation

All interactive elements—including the dark/light mode switcher, navigation bars, and weekly make buttons—are built with standard semantic HTML buttons and links with clear focus states to support keyboard-only navigation.
