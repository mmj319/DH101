🤖 How I Use AI

My personal philosophy and transparent methodology for working with AI in Digital Humanities.

My Philosophy on Artificial Intelligence

In Digital Humanities 101, artificial intelligence is treated neither as an unquestioned magic wand nor as an enemy of creativity. Instead, it is an object of critical inquiry—a collaborator, an imperfect mirror, and a cultural artifact that reflects the biases and structures of its training data.

🔍 Transparency & Prompt Disclosure

Whenever AI is employed in my critical makes or writing, it is thoroughly documented:

  • Exact Prompts: Recording the exact phrasing and iteration cycles passed to language or image models.
  • Attribution: Distinguishing machine outputs from human intervention, artistic editing, and curation.
  • Process Notes: Explaining why a tool was chosen and what limitations emerged during creation.

✋ Human Agency in the Loop

Generative AI can draft or suggest, but human hands provide intentionality, emotional nuance, and critical reflection. As explored in Week 3 (Selfie & Identity) and Week 4 (Comic & Storytelling), machine generations frequently smooth over human individuality or default to generic tropes. Active human intervention—such as hand-drawing comic panels or illustrating over AI outputs in IBIS Paint—is essential to reclaiming personal voice.

🌱 Critical Questions I Ask

  • Whose labor and creative works were scraped to build this model?
  • What societal norms or aesthetic biases is the model enforcing?
  • What is the environmental and carbon cost of running these inferences?
